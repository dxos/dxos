In this learning path we build one app, a contact book. In it, you give yourself a name, keep a few named spaces, and
invite people into them. Your contacts are the people you share a space with.

This page explains what a HALO identity is, and shows the finished contact book.

## What an identity is

In most apps, a person is an account that a server keeps. In HALO, a person is an identity, and everything the
contact book shows comes from one identity per person. An identity has four parts:

- **A DID.** Peers know the identity by this one name, on every device and in every space.
- **A profile.** The profile holds the identity's display name and any other data the app adds, such as an avatar.
  Every space the identity is in shows it.
- **Members.** An identity is a Keyhive group, and its members are the keys allowed to act for it. Each device holds its
  own key, so adding a device adds a member and removing one revokes it. The key behind a recovery code is a member too.
- **Spaces.** A space is a group as well, and the identity is one of its members, with an access level. The identity's
  devices act for it in every space it belongs to.

No server issues an identity. The device makes its keys. Each change to its members is a signed delegation or
revocation, and every peer checks it. [Identities and devices](/docs/halo/concepts/identities-devices/) describes the model in full.

## The finished app

Here is the contact book running for Alice, Bob and Carol. All three are in Book club, and Alice and Carol are also
in Climbing.

Open Contacts on Alice's device to see Bob and Carol with the spaces each shares with her. You can make a new space,
invite someone into it, or switch a device offline. MESH's [Presence](/docs/mesh/concepts/presence/) explains how
peers notice that a device has gone offline.

Each page of the learning path builds one piece of it:

| Page | Files | What it adds |
| --- | --- | --- |
| [Your profile](/docs/halo/learn/your-profile/) | `App.tsx`, `Welcome.tsx`, `ContactBook.tsx`, `Profile.tsx` | A client, an identity with a profile, and renaming |
| [Your spaces](/docs/halo/learn/your-spaces/) | `Spaces.tsx`, `ContactBook.tsx` | Named spaces, and a list to switch between them |
| [Invite people](/docs/halo/learn/invite-people/) | `Invitations.tsx` | Invite and Join, with an authorization code |
| [Your contacts](/docs/halo/learn/your-contacts/) | `Contacts.tsx` | Everyone you share a space with, and removing them |
| [Add a device](/docs/halo/learn/add-device/) | `Devices.tsx`, `Welcome.tsx` | Your phone, with the same name, spaces and contacts |
| [Recover an identity](/docs/halo/learn/recover-identity/) | `Recovery.tsx`, `Welcome.tsx` | A recovery code, and your identity back on a new device |

The code is Effect. Each change is an `Effect` that needs the HALO services, and a `HaloProvider` from
`@dxos/halo-react` holds each device's services.

A component reads state with that package's hooks, such as `useIdentity` and `useSpaces`. It runs a change with
`useHaloServices`, `Effect.provide` and `Effect.runPromise`.

Each piece sits in the component that uses it. The samples show it there, with the rest of the component folded away.
