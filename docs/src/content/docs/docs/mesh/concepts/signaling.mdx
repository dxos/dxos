Signaling is how a device learns which peers are on a **topic**, the key that names one group of devices. It is also
how the device exchanges the messages that open a direct link to one of those peers. EDGE is the signaling server.

## Model

Two browsers cannot open a connection to each other unaided. Neither knows the other's address, and most sit behind a
router that hides it. WebRTC needs a server that both can already reach to pass the first messages between them. That
server is the **signaling server**.

A space's topic is its space ID, which is the first 20 bytes of the SHA-256 of its space key. Every member of the
space, and EDGE, computes the same topic. An invitation has a topic of its own, carried in the invitation code.

On this page, a **peer** is another device on the same topic, and a **direct link** is a connection between two of
them. The direct links a device keeps for one topic are its swarm. EDGE is a peer too, but a device reaches it over
its WebSocket and needs no introduction.

**The signal manager.** A **signal manager** is the part of the client that talks to the signaling server. Each device
has one, and the `runtime.client.edgeFeatures.signaling` flag decides which:

- If the flag is on and the client has an EDGE URL, it is the EDGE signal manager, which sends everything over the
  device's [WebSocket to EDGE](/docs/edge/concepts/connecting/).
- Otherwise, the client uses an in-memory signal manager that only reaches peers in the same process. Tests use this
  one.

**Join, query and leave.** A device joins a topic to say it is there, queries the topic to list the peers on it, and
leaves when it closes the space. EDGE tells each peer on the topic when another joins or leaves.

**Offers, answers and signals.** To open a direct link, one peer sends an offer to another through EDGE, and the other
accepts or rejects it with an answer. The two then trade WebRTC signals until the link opens. From then on, nothing
for that link goes through signaling.

A signal is a session description or an ICE candidate, which is one address a device might be reached at.

**ICE and TURN.** **ICE**, short for Interactive Connectivity Establishment, is how WebRTC finds a route between two
devices from their candidates.

- Behind most home routers, a direct route exists.
- Behind strict NATs and firewalls, it does not. The link then goes through a **TURN** server, which relays the link's
  encrypted bytes. TURN is short for Traversal Using Relays around NAT.

## Configuration

| Setting                                  | Effect                                                                                     |
| ---------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| `runtime.client.edgeFeatures.signaling`  | Uses EDGE as the signaling server. Without it, devices in different processes never meet   |
| `runtime.services.ice`                   | A fixed list of ICE servers (STUN and TURN), passed straight to WebRTC                     |
| `runtime.services.iceProviders`          | URLs that return ICE servers, fetched once and cached. DXOS runs one at `https://dxos.network/ice` |

The configuration in [Installation](/docs/echo/learn/installation/) sets the signaling flag and the DXOS ICE provider.
See EDGE's [Configuration](/docs/edge/resources/configuration/) for the rest.

## Behavior

- Signaling carries only the messages that set up a direct link. Replication never goes through it.
- A device that loses its WebSocket keeps its open direct links. It cannot open new ones until it reconnects.

## Limits

- A TURN relay sees encrypted bytes only, but every byte of that link goes through it.
