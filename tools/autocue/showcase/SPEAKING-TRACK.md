## 1. Opening (0:00–0:04)

**On screen**

- The Composer ident: the DXOS mark resolving into the Composer wordmark.
- Light theme, which the first and third tours share.
- No interface yet: this is the title card.

**The framing it sets up** (for a longer cut, or the presenter's own intro)

- Composer is one workspace for documents, diagrams, data, projects, mail and meetings.
- It is local first: your data lives on your device and opens instantly.
- It works offline, and syncs peer to peer when you're connected.
- Many people can edit the same thing at once, with changes merging automatically.
- AI agents work inside it, as collaborators in the same spaces and threads.
- Everything is an object in one graph, so anything can link to anything.
- It is built from plugins, so it grows with what you need.
- It is open source, built on the DXOS platform.

## 6. Close (2:04–2:07)

**On screen**

- The end card: the Composer mark and its address.

**The takeaways it closes on** (for a longer cut, or questions afterwards)

- One workspace instead of a dozen apps: documents, diagrams, sheets, projects, mail, meetings.
- Your data stays yours, on your device, and works offline.
- Real-time collaboration without a central server owning the data.
- Agents that do real work: comments answered, tasks run, diagrams drawn, summaries written.
- Everything links: a deal, its emails, its meetings and its people are one graph.
- Plugins make it any shape you need, from a CRM to a chess club.
- Templates like DXOS Architecture and Alien Labs show a whole workspace in one click.
- Built on DXOS: ECHO for data, HALO for identity, EDGE for availability.
- Open source, so you can extend it and run it yourself.
